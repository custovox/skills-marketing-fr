# CRM plan method

## A strategic view of CRM

CRM is not a software project. Payne and Frow (2005) describe it as five cross-functional
processes: **strategy development** (which customers, which value), **value creation**
(what the customer and the company each gain), **multichannel integration** (consistent
experience across channels), **information management** (data, analytics, tools) and
**performance assessment** (indicators linked to shareholder and customer value). Use these
five processes as a checklist: a plan that only lists e-mails covers one of them.

## Segmentation

### RFM

Score each customer on **R**ecency (days since last purchase), **F**requency (number of
orders over a period) and **M**onetary value (revenue over the period), usually in quintiles
(1-5). RFM is simple, explainable and strongly linked to future value; Fader, Hardie and Lee
(2005) show how recency and frequency together predict customer lifetime value.

Typical RFM-based segments: champions (high R, F, M), loyal, promising newcomers, at risk
(low R, high F), hibernating, lost.

### Loyalty is not always profitable

Reinartz and Kumar (2000) found that long-life customers are not necessarily the most
profitable in a non-contractual setting. Check margin and cost-to-serve before investing in
a segment, and do not equate duration with value.

### Lifecycle

Prospect → new customer (first order) → active → loyal → at risk → lapsed. Lifecycle stages
give natural triggers for scenarios.

## Key indicators

| Indicator | Definition |
|---|---|
| Repeat purchase rate | Share of customers with at least 2 orders over a period (e.g. 12 months) |
| Second-purchase rate at N days | Share of new customers who reorder within N days |
| Churn (non-contractual) | Share of active customers with no purchase beyond their usual cycle (e.g. 2x the median inter-purchase time) |
| Customer lifetime value (CLV) | Expected margin generated by a customer over the relationship; a simple version: average margin per order x orders per year x expected years |
| Uplift | Difference in behaviour between targeted customers and a control group that did not receive the scenario |
| Pressure | Marketing messages received per customer per week/month |
| Deliverability health | Open/click rates, unsubscribe rate, spam complaints |

## GDPR and electronic marketing (EU, France)

General guidance, to be confirmed with the company's data protection officer:
- **Consumers (B2C)**: commercial e-mails and SMS require **prior consent**. Exception
  (soft opt-in): existing customers may receive messages about **similar products or
  services** from the same company if they were informed and able to object when their
  details were collected.
- **Professionals (B2B)**: messages related to the person's job are possible without prior
  consent, if the person was informed and can object.
- **Every message**: identify the sender and offer a simple, free opt-out.
- **Service messages** (order confirmation, delivery, recall or safety information) are not
  marketing and do not depend on marketing consent.
- **Data minimisation and retention**: keep only data needed for the scenarios; define how
  long inactive prospects and customers are kept.
The French data protection authority (CNIL) publishes practical guides on these rules.

## Scenario design rules

1. **One trigger, one objective.** A scenario starts from an event (order, delivery, date,
   inactivity, ticket closed) and aims at one behaviour.
2. **Exclusions matter as much as targets**: exclude customers with an open complaint, a
   recent purchase, or no consent.
3. **Service before sales**: advice, follow-up and problem resolution build the
   relationship; discounts train customers to wait for discounts.
4. **Measure uplift** with a random control group (5-10 %).
5. **Start small**: 3 scenarios that run well beat 15 that nobody maintains.
