## 1. Diagnostic quiz (extract: 8 of 12 questions)

**Q1 - Inbound foundations - understand**
Maison Verveine publishes a guide "How to build an evening skincare routine for dry skin"
and promotes it with a small paid social campaign. Is this inbound marketing?
- A. No, because paid media is outbound by definition.
- B. Yes, because the content answers a question the target is actively asking; paid media
  only amplifies it.
- C. Yes, because any content published online is inbound.
- D. No, because inbound only uses SEO.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Inbound is defined by the logic of attracting people with something useful they
look for, not by the channel. **A** confuses channel and approach. **C** is too broad:
content that interrupts or is irrelevant is not inbound. **D** is false: social media,
e-mail and conversations are part of inbound too.
</details>

**Q2 - Flywheel - analyse**
Since July, Maison Verveine's customers complain about late parcels and unreachable
customer service. In flywheel terms, what is happening?
- A. The brand has lost force in the attract phase.
- B. Friction has increased in the delight phase, slowing the whole flywheel.
- C. The funnel has become too narrow at the decision stage.
- D. Nothing: delivery is a logistics issue, outside the flywheel.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Late deliveries and unreachable service are friction after the purchase; unhappy
customers stop recommending and reordering, which removes energy from the whole
flywheel. **A**: attraction is not the problem described. **C** uses the funnel vocabulary
and the wrong stage. **D** is the classic mistake: everything the customer experiences
counts, whichever department owns it.
</details>

**Q3 - Buyer's journey - apply**
A visitor searches "rougeurs après crème de nuit que faire". Which journey stage and which
content fits best?
- A. Decision - a 15 % discount code.
- B. Awareness - an article explaining possible causes of redness and when to see a
  professional.
- C. Consideration - a comparison of night creams by brand.
- D. Decision - a free trial of the Crème Nuit.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The person is trying to understand a problem: awareness stage, educational content.
**A** and **D** push a purchase too early (and here, a sensitive health-related query
calls for caution). **C** fits a person who has already defined the problem and compares
solutions.
</details>

**Q4 - Lifecycle stages - understand** (true/false + justification)
"A contact who downloaded the refill-interest survey results is automatically a sales
qualified lead."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**False.** A download shows interest (lead, possibly marketing qualified depending on the
agreed rules). A sales qualified lead requires the sales team to accept the contact as
ready for a sales conversation, according to criteria agreed between marketing and
sales.
</details>

**Q5 - Conversion path - apply**
Put the elements of Maison Verveine's "skin diagnostic" conversion path in order:
thank-you page · call to action in a blog article · follow-up e-mail with routine advice ·
landing page with the diagnostic form.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Call to action → landing page with form → thank-you page → follow-up e-mail. The thank-you
page delivers what was promised and suggests a next step; the e-mail continues the
relationship.
</details>

**Q6 - Lead scoring - analyse**
Which pair of criteria best combines **fit** and **engagement** for Maison Verveine's B2B
offer to partner organic stores?
- A. Opened 3 newsletters · visited the careers page.
- B. Store sells natural cosmetics and has more than 2 outlets · requested the wholesale
  price list.
- C. Lives in Drôme · likes posts on social media.
- D. Bought the Coffret Découverte · downloaded the brand logo.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Fit = the organisation matches the target (sells natural cosmetics, size);
engagement = a buying signal (asks for prices). **A** has two weak engagement signals, one
irrelevant (careers). **C** and **D** mix consumer behaviour with a B2B offer.
</details>

**Q7 - E-mail - understand**
Maison Verveine's open rates drop after it sends to a purchased list of 20,000 addresses.
Main reason?
- A. The subject lines were too short.
- B. Sending to people who never opted in damages engagement and sender reputation, and
  raises consent issues.
- C. Purchased lists contain only B2B addresses.
- D. Sending times were wrong.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Without permission, recipients ignore, delete or report messages, which harms
deliverability for the whole base, and in the EU the brand would lack a valid basis to
send consumer marketing e-mails. **A** and **D** can matter but are not the main cause.
**C** is false.
</details>

**Q8 - Workflows - apply**
Design the enrolment trigger and the first two actions of a replenishment workflow for the
Crème Nuit.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Trigger: contact has an order containing the Crème Nuit, 80 days ago, and no later order;
exclusions: open ticket, no marketing consent. Actions: (1) send the "time to restock?"
e-mail with the usual basket; (2) after 7 days, if the e-mail was opened and no order was
placed, send a reminder; otherwise end. Measure against a control group.
</details>

**Diagnostic result (illustrative)**: strong on inbound foundations and journey; weaker on
lifecycle stages, lead scoring and workflows → plan starts with CRM concepts.

## 2. Revision sheet: lifecycle stages

- **In one sentence**: the lifecycle stage tells everyone where a contact stands in the
  relationship, so that marketing, sales and service act consistently.
- **Why it matters**: avoids calling a contact too early, or sending prospecting e-mails to
  a loyal customer; supports reporting of conversion between stages.
- **How it works**:
  1. Stages go from first interest to advocacy (typically subscriber → lead → MQL → SQL →
     opportunity → customer → evangelist).
  2. Movement is based on explicit rules (forms, score, sales acceptance, closed deal).
  3. Stages normally move forward; moving backwards is a deliberate exception.
  4. Marketing and sales agree on MQL and SQL definitions (service level agreement).
- **Example**: a store manager downloads the wholesale catalogue (lead), reaches the agreed
  score (MQL), is accepted by the sales manager after a call (SQL), receives a quote
  (opportunity), places a first order (customer), then recommends the brand to another
  store (evangelist).
- **Common confusions**: lifecycle stage ≠ deal stage (a deal moves through a pipeline);
  MQL ≠ SQL (marketing vs sales judgement); a download alone ≠ readiness to buy.
- **Self-test**:
  1. Who decides that an MQL becomes an SQL?
  2. Give one rule that would move a contact from lead to MQL at Maison Verveine.
  3. Why should stages rarely move backwards?

## 3. Case study: setting up inbound at Maison Verveine (group work, 90 min)

**Context**: Maison Verveine wants to (1) win back loyal customers after a difficult summer
and (2) recruit 30 new partner organic stores in 2027. It has three consumer personas (P1
The Committed Naturalist, P2 The Results-Driven Skin Fixer, P3 The Occasional Gift Buyer)
and a CRM it barely uses.

**Questions**
1. For goal 2 (stores), write a B2B buyer persona in 6 lines and its buyer's journey (one
   question per stage).
2. Propose one piece of content per journey stage and its conversion path.
3. Define the lifecycle stage rules from lead to SQL, and a simple lead score (fit and
   engagement, 4-6 criteria).
4. For goal 1, describe two workflows (trigger, actions, exclusions, KPI).
5. Name two sources of friction in Maison Verveine's flywheel and one action for each.

**Scoring guide (20 points)**: persona grounded in stated hypotheses (3) · journey and
content consistent by stage (4) · conversion path complete (2) · lifecycle rules and score
explicit and justified (4) · workflows with trigger, exclusions and KPI (4) · flywheel
friction correctly identified (3).

**Elements of a model answer**: B2B persona "independent organic store manager" seeking
products that sell and margin, short on time; awareness content "5 skincare trends in
organic stores", consideration "margin and sell-out simulator", decision "starter kit offer
+ testimonials"; MQL = fit score ≥ 60 and catalogue download; SQL = accepted after a
qualification call; workflows = replenishment and service recovery (see the `plan-crm`
example); frictions = late deliveries (carrier plan), unreachable service (callback,
self-service tracking).
